I am an economist working on health care access and use during crises. My research uses administrative hospital records and household surveys, with methods from applied microeconometrics, to study who gets care when health systems come under pressure from pandemics and war.

I have completed my DPhil at the University of Oxford (viva pending). Before Oxford, I studied at University College London and the College of Europe.

Alongside my research, I am a strategy consultant at the Tony Blair Institute for Global Change, where I advise governments in Europe on economic, health and digital policy. I previously worked as an economist at the Belgian Ministry of Economy.

Working papers

  • War exposure and health care utilisation: Evidence from Ukraine

    This paper studies how regional exposure to the full-scale invasion of Ukraine changed the use of health care. It compares 2019 and 2023 waves of the nationally representative Health Index. Ukraine survey in a difference-in-differences design that interacts the post-invasion period with oblast-level war intensity. Outcomes include inpatient stays, unmet need for hospitalisation, care-seeking, diagnostics, medicines received and out-of-pocket payments.

  • Inequalities in critical care use during COVID-19: Ethnicity, age, sex and deprivation

    Uses linked Hospital Episode Statistics admitted patient care and adult critical care records for England, 2018/19 to 2021/22, to document how critical care use differed across patient groups before and during the pandemic.

  • Dementia and critical care admission during COVID-19

    Examines critical care admission for hospital patients living with dementia and frailty in England during the pandemic, using linked Hospital Episode Statistics records.

  • Hospital variation and workload in critical care during COVID-19

    Studies variation across English hospitals in critical care admission and how it relates to workload during the pandemic.
